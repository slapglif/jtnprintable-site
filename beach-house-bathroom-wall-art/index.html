How to plan a beach house bathroom gallery wall

Start with the wall that stays driest and is easiest to see from the doorway. A vertical pair works well beside a vanity or linen cabinet, while three coordinated prints can balance a longer wall above towel hooks. Leave comfortable space around mirrors, sconces, and trim so the fish and coral details remain readable instead of competing with bathroom fixtures.

Choose frames for humid rooms

For a frequently used bathroom, print behind acrylic or glass and use frames with secure backing. Keep artwork away from direct shower spray and standing condensation. White, pale oak, navy, or natural rattan-look frames reinforce a coastal palette without making the room feel themed.

Pick a practical print size

Measure the open wall area first, then subtract the spacing you want between frames. Small prints suit a powder room or narrow wall; larger coordinated pieces create a focal point in a beach-house guest bath. Print one inexpensive draft page before ordering final paper or frames to confirm scale and color in the room's actual light.

Is anything shipped?

What paper works for coastal wall art?

Heavy matte paper reduces glare near mirrors and usually gives illustrated coral and fish a clean finish. A local printer can recommend stock for your chosen frame size.
