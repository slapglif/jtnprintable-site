Choose a print size for your wall

Start with the furniture below the display rather than the total wall width. For a six-print grid, keep the complete framed arrangement narrower than the desk, console, or sofa anchoring it. The included 2:3 sizes—such as 8×12, 12×18, and 20×30 inches—make it easier to scale every storm scene consistently. A-series files are also included for A4 through A1 frames.

Paper and frame choices for dark storm prints

Matte or lightly textured fine-art paper reduces reflections in rooms with monitors or directional lighting, while satin photo paper can emphasize cloud contrast. Black frames reinforce a cinematic game-room look; natural wood frames soften the palette for an office or living space. Print one smaller test file first if shadow detail is important, because screen brightness can make dark areas appear lighter than the final print.

How to build a storm-themed game room wall

For a game room or streaming backdrop, place the six-print grid on a wall that stays visible behind the seating area rather than directly behind a bright monitor. Keep the center of the arrangement near seated eye level and leave an even gap between frames. Charcoal, black, or dark bronze frames connect the supercell palette with typical gaming furniture without adding another competing color.

If the room already has LED accent lighting, use a neutral white setting when choosing paper and checking the first test print. Colored LEDs can shift gray clouds toward blue, purple, or green. A matte test print at 8×12 inches is a low-commitment way to inspect shadow detail before ordering all six at a larger size.
